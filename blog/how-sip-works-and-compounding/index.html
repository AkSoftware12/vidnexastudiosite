How a SIP Works: Compounding Explained Simply

Understand what a SIP is, how compounding grows regular monthly investments over time, and how to use a SIP calculator sensibly.

Calculators · 2 min read · Updated 4 November 2026

In this guide
  1. What compounding means
  2. How the SIP Calculator works
  3. What the calculator cannot tell you
  4. Sensible ways to use it

A systematic investment plan, or SIP, lets you invest a fixed amount at regular intervals, usually every month, in a mutual fund. Instead of trying to pick the perfect day to invest, you invest steadily. Over time the money you invest and the returns on it can grow through compounding.

What compounding means

Compounding means you earn returns on your earlier returns as well as on the money you put in. If you invest ₹5,000 a month and the investment grows at an assumed 12% a year, the growth in later years is applied to a larger and larger base. That is why the same SIP looks much bigger after 20 years than twice the value after 10 years.

How the SIP Calculator works

You enter the monthly amount, an expected yearly return and the number of years. The tool treats each monthly instalment as growing for the remaining months and adds them up. It then shows the total you invested, the estimated gain and the final value. The calculator is only an illustration of the maths.

What the calculator cannot tell you

  • Market-linked returns rise and fall. No return is guaranteed.
  • Actual returns will not be the same every year as the calculator assumes.
  • Costs, taxes and inflation reduce what you finally keep and what it can buy.

Sensible ways to use it

  • Try a cautious return rate as well as an optimistic one.
  • Compare the same monthly amount for 10, 15 and 20 years to see how time matters.
  • Try increasing the SIP a little every year, a step-up, to see how it speeds up your goal.
  • Match the investment period to your goal. Money needed soon should not usually sit in risky investments.

This article is for general education and is not investment advice. Speak to a qualified adviser before investing.

Frequently asked questions

Is the SIP result guaranteed?

No. It only shows what would happen if the entered return were earned every year.

Is it better to start early?

Starting early gives compounding more time to work, which usually matters more than the exact return rate.
