Cricket Batting Game: How to Time Your Shots for Sixes

How the timing works in our Cricket Batting game, what gets you out, and how to chase a target with 3 wickets in 2 overs.

Games · 1 min read · Updated 4 November 2026

Our Cricket Batting game is all about timing. The ball comes down the pitch and you tap once when it reaches the bat. Here is how the timing zones work and how to score more.

The timing zones

  • Perfect, right in the yellow zone: six runs.
  • Slightly early or late: four runs.
  • A bit more off: two runs if early, one run if late.
  • Clearly early or late: a dot ball, no run.
  • Far too early: caught. Far too late, or no swing at all: bowled.

Modes

  • 2 overs: 12 balls, 3 wickets, set a high score.
  • 5 overs: 30 balls, 3 wickets, a longer innings.
  • Chase: beat a random target in 12 balls with 3 wickets.

Tips

  • Watch the ball, not the bowler. The speed changes from ball to ball.
  • In Chase mode, count what you need. If 8 runs are needed from 4 balls, two fours are enough. Do not swing wildly for a six on every ball.
  • On Hard the window is small and the ball is fast, so wait a fraction longer than feels natural.
  • Three boundaries in a row show a streak. Keep it going for bragging rights, not extra runs.

Frequently asked questions

How many wickets do I get?

Three in every mode.

Does the game work with a keyboard?

Yes. Press Space or Enter to bowl and to hit.

Is my best score saved?

Yes, separately for each mode and bowling level, in your own browser.
