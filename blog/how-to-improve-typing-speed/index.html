How to Improve Your Typing Speed: Practical Tips and a Free Test

Learn what a good typing speed is, how WPM is measured, and the habits that raise speed and accuracy with a few minutes of daily practice.

Text · 1 min read · Updated 4 November 2026

In this guide

  2. What is a good speed?
  3. Habits that help
  4. Using the Word Counter for writing practice

Typing faster saves time every day, for studying, writing and office work. Speed comes from accuracy and good habits, not from rushing.

How WPM is measured

Typing tests count every five characters, including spaces, as one word. Words per minute is that word count divided by the time taken. Accuracy is the share of characters you typed correctly. A fast score with many mistakes is not useful, so look at both numbers.

What is a good speed?

  • Around 35 to 45 WPM is typical for most people.
  • Above 60 WPM is fast for office work.
  • 70 WPM and above is common for professional typists.

Habits that help

  1. Learn touch typing: keep your fingers on the home row and avoid looking at the keys.
  2. Prioritise accuracy. Slow down until mistakes are rare, then build speed.
  3. Practise 10 to 15 minutes every day rather than one long session a week.
  4. Sit upright with relaxed shoulders and wrists to avoid strain.
  5. Take the Typing Speed Test regularly and track your progress.

Using the Word Counter for writing practice

After a typing session, paste your writing into the Word Counter to see words, characters and reading time. Setting a daily word target is a good way to build the habit.

Progress is usually steady: many learners add 5 to 10 WPM in the first few weeks of regular practice, though results differ from person to person.

Frequently asked questions

Does a bigger keyboard or phone change my WPM?

Yes. Phone typing is usually slower than a physical keyboard, so compare scores on the same device.

How often should I practise?

A short daily session is more effective than occasional long ones.
