How to Calculate EMI: Formula, Example and Ways to Reduce It

Learn how loan EMI is calculated, see a worked example with the formula, and find simple ways to lower your monthly instalment and total interest.

Calculators · 2 min read · Updated 4 November 2026

In this guide

  2. A worked example
  3. Why the first EMIs are mostly interest
  4. How to lower your EMI or total interest
  5. Fixed and floating rates

EMI stands for equated monthly instalment. It is the fixed amount you pay to the lender every month until a home loan, car loan or personal loan is fully repaid. Each EMI contains two parts: interest on the outstanding balance and a repayment of the principal. Knowing how it is calculated helps you compare loans before you sign.

The EMI formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1)

  • P is the loan amount (principal).
  • r is the monthly interest rate, which is the yearly rate divided by 12 and by 100.
  • n is the number of monthly instalments.

A worked example

Take a loan of ₹5,00,000 at 10% a year for 5 years. The monthly rate r is 10 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.008333 and n is 60. Putting these in the formula gives an EMI of about ₹10,624. Over 60 months you pay roughly ₹6,37,400 in total, so the interest is about ₹1,37,400. You can check this in seconds with the EMI Calculator.

Why the first EMIs are mostly interest

Interest is charged on the balance still owed. At the start the balance is large, so most of the EMI goes to interest. As the balance falls, a bigger share goes to principal. This is why the yearly schedule in the calculator shows the balance falling slowly at first and faster later.

How to lower your EMI or total interest

  • Choose a longer tenure to lower the monthly EMI, but remember that total interest rises.
  • Make a larger down payment so the principal is smaller.
  • Compare interest rates and processing fees from several lenders.
  • Part-prepay when you have spare money. Check first whether your lender charges a prepayment fee.
  • Keep a good credit score, since it can help you get a lower rate.

Fixed and floating rates

With a fixed rate the EMI stays the same. With a floating rate the lender can change the rate, so your EMI or your tenure can change. The calculator assumes one fixed rate for the whole loan, so treat the result as a close estimate.

This article is for general information and is not financial advice. Check the final figures in your loan agreement.

Frequently asked questions

Is a lower EMI always better?

Not always. A lower EMI usually comes from a longer tenure, which means you pay more interest overall.

Does the EMI Calculator include processing fees?

No. It uses only the principal, rate and tenure, so add fees and insurance yourself when comparing offers.
