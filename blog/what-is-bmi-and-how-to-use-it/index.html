What Is BMI? How It Is Calculated and What It Can and Cannot Tell You

Understand BMI, the formula, the standard ranges and why it is only a rough screening number, not a full measure of your health.

Calculators · 1 min read · Updated 4 November 2026

In this guide

  2. The standard ranges for adults
  3. What BMI cannot show
  4. Using the BMI Calculator

BMI, or body mass index, is a number that relates your weight to your height. Doctors use it as a quick screening tool. It is simple to calculate but it has clear limits, so it should never be the only thing you look at.

The formula

BMI = weight in kilograms ÷ (height in metres)². A person who weighs 70 kg and is 1.75 m tall has a BMI of 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.9.

The standard ranges for adults

  • Below 18.5: underweight
  • 18.5 to 24.9: normal range
  • 25 to 29.9: overweight
  • 30 and above: obese

Some health guidelines for South Asian adults use lower cut-offs for overweight and obesity because health risks can rise at a lower BMI. A doctor can tell you which guidance applies to you.

What BMI cannot show

  • It does not separate muscle from fat, so a muscular person may show a high BMI.
  • It does not show where fat is stored on the body.
  • It does not account for age, sex or medical conditions in the simple formula.
  • It is not a diagnosis.

Using the BMI Calculator

Enter your height and weight in the units you prefer. The tool shows your BMI, the range it falls in and a weight range that matches the normal BMI band for your height. Use it as a starting point for a conversation with a doctor or dietitian.

This article is general information and not medical advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

Not always. Muscle weighs more than fat, so athletes can have a high BMI without excess body fat.

Can children use the same BMI ranges?

No. Children's BMI is compared with charts for age and sex, so ask a paediatrician.
