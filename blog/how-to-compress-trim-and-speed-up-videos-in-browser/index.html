How to Compress, Trim and Change Video Speed in Your Browser

Learn how to make a video smaller, cut unwanted parts and create slow-motion or fast-forward clips without installing an app or uploading the file.

Video & Audio · 1 min read · Updated 4 November 2026

In this guide

  2. Step 1: check the video
  3. Step 2: trim it
  4. Step 3: compress it
  5. Optional: change the speed
  6. Tips

Sharing a big video on WhatsApp or a form with an upload limit is a common problem. Three simple edits fix most cases: trim it, compress it and, if you like, change the speed.

How browser video editing works

The browser plays your video and re-records it as a new file while it applies the change, for example a smaller size or a different speed. Because this happens in real time on your device, a 2 minute video takes about 2 minutes. Nothing is uploaded.

Step 1: check the video

Use Video Info to see the resolution, duration, size and bitrate. A 1080p video of a few minutes can be hundreds of megabytes.

Step 2: trim it

Open the Video Trimmer, choose the start and end points and preview the section. Cutting unneeded seconds reduces size immediately.

Step 3: compress it

Open the Video Compressor and choose a lower resolution or quality. Reducing resolution, for example from 1080p to 720p, saves the most space and is usually fine for phones.

Optional: change the speed

The Video Speed Changer can slow a clip down to 0.25x for slow motion or speed it up to 4x for a time-lapse effect. Sound speeds up or slows down with the picture.

Tips

  • Keep the browser tab open and visible until the file is ready.
  • Test with a short clip to find the best settings.
  • Trim first, then compress.
  • Use a desktop browser such as Chrome or Edge for the best results.
  • Only edit videos you own or have permission to use.

Frequently asked questions

Why does compressing take as long as the video?

The browser records the video again in real time, so processing time is about the length of the video.

Which output format do I get?

It depends on your browser, usually WebM or MP4.
